UnPaws: mood board v0.1

Three directions for the look, all still built from real-looking interfaces. Each one gives the game a believable "device" for the honest timer, a place for the cue to live, a new realistic trickster, and room for the 2103 lore to leak through. These are static mockups; nothing in the game has changed yet.

Direction A

TAPE: 80s VHS, Kung Fury energy

You're watching an archived tape on a CRT inside a neon synthwave world. Chrome logo, laser grid, a worn-tape picture with tracking bars, and the VCR's own on-screen display saying PLAY ▶ / PAUSE ❚❚.

Palette & type
Chrome Orbitron for the logo and cues, VT323 for the VCR display and tape counter.
Gameplay fit
  • Honest timer = the VCR tape counter.
  • Levels 1–2 show the PLAY/PAUSE display; later levels hide it.
  • New trickster: a paused VHS frame really does jitter and show a noise bar. That's the "drift while paused" idea from v1, but realistic this time.
Lore fit
  • Footage of physical life as "archived tapes".
  • Loud and fun on the surface, which makes the dark lore land harder.
  • Risk: high energy can drown the subtle hints.
Direction B

DESKTOP: vaporwave operating system

The whole screen is the unit's desktop: a 90s-style OS in vaporwave pastels, a media player window, a cue that arrives as a system dialog, desktop icons and a taskbar that quietly tell the story.

Palette & type
Classic 90s system font (Tahoma), VT323 for the cue word. Beveled grey windows on top of pastel vaporwave.
Gameplay fit
  • Honest timer = the player's time display plus the taskbar clock.
  • New tricksters, all realistic: the window goes "(Not Responding)" and the frame hangs; a screensaver kicks in; notifications pop up over the video.
  • Opens the door to the "whole desktop" idea you mentioned.
Lore fit
  • The strongest of the three: it is the unit's terminal.
  • Icons, file names and the tray carry hints without any text dump ("rest_request.bat", "rest queued").
  • The operator can literally message you.
Direction C

CONSOLE: 70s retro-future corporate

Warm 70s: a wood-grain TV set, groovy stripes, chunky type, and an amber terminal for the operator, like the ship computers in 70s sci-fi. Cosier on the surface and quietly creepier.

Palette & type
Shrikhand/Righteous for titles, VT323 in amber for the operator terminal, label-maker strips for metadata.
Gameplay fit
  • Honest timer = the counter in the TV's corner.
  • New tricksters: a power dip that dims the picture, CRT picture wobble, channel noise.
  • Less "YouTube", more physical object.
Lore fit
  • Strong "the company owns you" feeling: amber terminal, labelled reels, cold operator lines.
  • 70s optimism about the future, viewed from 2103, is a nice irony.
  • Furthest from the vaporwave reference.

My recommendation: B as the frame, A inside it

Use the vaporwave desktop as the world, since it's the unit's terminal, carries the lore through icons and dialogs, and leaves room for the bigger "whole desktop" experience. Then make the videos themselves look like Kung Fury-style VHS tapes: neon, tracking bars, VCR display. That gives us a realistic "paused but jittering" trickster to replace the film grain.

C's amber operator terminal could appear as one dialog style inside B, for when the operators speak, so the 70s note isn't lost.
